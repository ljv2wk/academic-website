Working projects

Political Institutional Trust and Mental Health: Longitudinal Evidence from Multiple National Panels

This project examines the relationship between political institutional trust and self-reported mental health across multiple national survey datasets. Using longitudinal analyses, I investigate the direction of this relationship and whether it differs across measures of mental health and subjective well-being. The project builds on research presented at the 2023 APSA Political Psychology Pre-Conference.

Weaponizing Public Health for Political Gain: A Mental Health Status Threat Survey Experiment

A survey-experimental investigation of how policy threats targeting people with mental-health conditions may influence political attitudes, emotions, and engagement. Part of the book project.

Choosing to Speak or Type: Response Mode Choice Across Open-Ended Survey Questions

This methodological project examines how the option to speak rather than type responses to open-ended survey questions shapes participation, response quality, and the measurement of public opinion. It explores the implications of response mode and mode choice for survey research, including respondents’ experiences with speech-to-text technology.

Neurodivergence, Mental Health, and the Boundaries of Political Identity

Building on my research on mental health and disability identity, this project examines neurodivergence as a social and political identity, including how people understand and identify with neurodivergence and how question wording affects its measurement. The project draws in part on survey questions obtained through the 2025 and 2026 AAPOR question competitions.
